#### **Scene 3.1**

1. **In data analysis, what does "hallucination" specifically refer to?**  
   a) An AI refusing to answer any question involving numbers  
   b) A chart rendering with the wrong colours  
   c) A dataset that has been encrypted before upload  
   d) An AI stating a number, trend, or cause that is not actually supported by the data it was given  

   **Answer:** d) An AI stating a number, trend, or cause that is not actually supported by the data it was given  

   **Explanation:** Hallucination in data analysis means confidently stating something — a wrong average, an invented trend, an unsupported cause — that the source data does not actually back up.

2. **A dataset shows that students who join a study group also tend to get higher grades. Which statement is the safest to write in a report?**  
   a) "Joining a study group is associated with higher grades in this data; a causal link is not yet proven."  
   b) "Joining a study group causes higher grades."  
   c) "Grades have nothing to do with study groups."  
   d) "Every student must join a study group starting next term."  

   **Answer:** a) "Joining a study group is associated with higher grades in this data; a causal link is not yet proven."  

   **Explanation:** The data shows a correlation. Students who join study groups might already be more motivated for other reasons — writing "causes" without further testing overstates what the data can prove.

3. **An AI claims a defect rate of "about 20%" after being told 18 out of 675 units failed inspection. What should you do before repeating that number?**  
   a) Trust it immediately — AI arithmetic is always correct  
   b) Recompute it yourself: 18 ÷ 675 ≈ 2.7%, and flag the large discrepancy  
   c) Round it up further to 25% to be safe  
   d) Remove the defect metric from the report entirely rather than check it  

   **Answer:** b) Recompute it yourself: 18 ÷ 675 ≈ 2.7%, and flag the large discrepancy  

   **Explanation:** 18 ÷ 675 is approximately 2.7%, not 20% — a sanity check that takes seconds and prevents a small, real number from being reported as something far more alarming.

4. **A backend approval step takes 48 hours on average before a user can proceed. A teammate says, "This 48-hour wait must be a broken screen in the app — let's redesign it." What does validating domain logic suggest here?**  
   a) Agree immediately and redesign the screen the user sees during the wait  
   b) Assume the AI invented the 48-hour figure and ignore it  
   c) Check whether the 48 hours is actually a backend review process rather than a UI problem before proposing a redesign  
   d) Domain logic is irrelevant once a number has been verified  

   **Answer:** c) Check whether the 48 hours is actually a backend review process rather than a UI problem before proposing a redesign  

   **Explanation:** A number can be arithmetically correct and still be misdiagnosed. Validating domain logic means asking whether the proposed cause actually matches how the real process works — a backend review delay is not fixed by a screen redesign.

5. **Which of these is a genuinely "decision-ready" recommendation in a report?**  
   a) "We should improve customer retention."  
   b) "Marketing should try harder."  
   c) "The data looks interesting; more research is needed."  
   d) "Send a one-tap loyalty-signup prompt to every customer within 48 hours of their first purchase, and track signup rate over the next four weeks."  

   **Answer:** d) "Send a one-tap loyalty-signup prompt to every customer within 48 hours of their first purchase, and track signup rate over the next four weeks."  

   **Explanation:** A decision-ready recommendation names a specific, buildable action and a way to check whether it worked. The other options are vague enough that no team could act on them directly.
