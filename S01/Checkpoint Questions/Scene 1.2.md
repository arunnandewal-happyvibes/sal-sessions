#### **Scene 1.2**

1. **Why should you aggregate a large raw dataset before pasting it into an AI chat tool, rather than pasting every row?**  
   a) AI tools refuse to read any table with more than ten rows  
   b) Aggregation removes the need to ever verify the AI's output afterward  
   c) Raw rows must always be converted to images before an AI can read them  
   d) A small, clean, aggregated table is easier to check and produces a more focused summary  

   **Answer:** d) A small, clean, aggregated table is easier to check and produces a more focused summary  

   **Explanation:** Shrinking data to the question makes both the prompt and the answer easier to verify. It does not remove the need for verification, and AI tools can technically read long tables — the point is usefulness, not a hard limit.

2. **A weekly attendance summary states only "average attendance was 85%." What is missing for this to count as a complete summary?**  
   a) Nothing — one overall number is always a complete summary  
   b) The macro pattern (is it rising, falling, flat) and any anomalous days  
   c) The exact seating chart for every classroom  
   d) A prediction of next year's attendance  

   **Answer:** b) The macro pattern (is it rising, falling, flat) and any anomalous days  

   **Explanation:** A complete summary needs the overall number plus the shape of the trend and any standout points — a single average alone leaves out two of the three required elements.

3. **An AI states a shop's weekend sales "tripled," based on sales going from 3 items to 9 items in one day. What is the most useful next question to ask before repeating this claim?**  
   a) None — "tripled" is mathematically correct, so it is safe to repeat as-is  
   b) Whether the AI used a friendly tone while phrasing the claim  
   c) Whether the sample size (3 to 9 items) is large enough for "tripled" to mean anything  
   d) Whether the shop's logo appeared in the chart  

   **Answer:** c) Whether the sample size (3 to 9 items) is large enough for "tripled" to mean anything  

   **Explanation:** The arithmetic is technically correct, but a jump from 3 to 9 items is too small a sample to treat as a real trend — checking the sample size behind a percentage change is a core evaluation habit.
